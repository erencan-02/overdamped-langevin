Potential SDE

Every particle follows the overdamped Langevin equation dXt = −∇Ψ(Xt) dt + σ dWt It slides downhill on the landscape Ψ while Brownian motion with diffusivity σ shakes it. warm areas are hills (Ψ > 0) and push particles away, cool areas are valleys (Ψ < 0) and attract them. |Ψ| is capped at . The force field toggle draws the drift −∇Ψ as arrows: they point the way particles are pushed, and their length grows with the force and levels off for very steep slopes.

Brush mode

  • Hold right mouse to lower Ψ
  • The brush is a Gaussian: the circles show 1σ and 2σ

Particle mode

Numerical schemes. Euler–Maruyama: Xn+1 = Xn − ∇Ψ(Xn)Δt + σΔW, one force evaluation per step. Stochastic Heun: an Euler–Maruyama predictor X̃ followed by Xn+1 = Xn − ½[∇Ψ(Xn) + ∇Ψ(X̃)]Δt + σΔW with the same ΔW, two force evaluations per step. With additive noise both have strong order 1, but Heun is second order in the drift (and reported weak order 2), so it is noticeably more accurate where the force dominates. Sub-steps are 2 ms of simulated time (up to 10 ms when thousands of particles share the frame budget). Ψ lives on a grid with spacing h = 0.04; its gradient uses central differences and is interpolated continuously between grid nodes.
